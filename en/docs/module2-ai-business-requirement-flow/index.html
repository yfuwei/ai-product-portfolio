01｜Business Problem

The problem is not a lack of feedback,but that signals never become organizational action.

Sales feedback, after-sales records, CRM notes, regional meetings, and quality issues already contain many business signals. The bottleneck is not whether information exists. It is that signals lose evidence, context, ownership, and status as they move through summaries, handoffs, routing, and review.

Information exists, but that does not make it a valid requirement; feedback is recorded, but that does not mean the organization will act.

02｜Through Example / User Task

Before generating a requirement,first ask whether these signals point to the same issue.

Take “back-row space feels small / family ride feels uncomfortable” as the example. It does not appear as a requirement at first. It shows up across sales conversations, CRM notes, after-sales feedback, and regional meetings. The first product judgment is not to ask AI to generate a requirement, but to identify whether these scattered expressions point to the same business signal.

A candidate card is not the result. It is only the starting point for the confirmation gate.

03｜Readiness Judgment

A candidate is not a result，It enters the workflow only after passing the gates.

The candidate signal generated by AI is still a to-be-confirmed object. Before it enters the workflow, the team must judge whether the problem is valid, whether evidence is sufficient, whether organizational action is needed, and whether it is suitable for a small pilot.

Not every candidate should move forward. Only those that pass the gates enter organizational action.

04｜Before & After AI Intervention

Where does AI actually intervene?What does it change, and what does it not own?

AI intervenes at the front of the flow: it turns scattered feedback from sales, CRM, after-sales, and meetings into confirmable Candidate Signal Cards. It changes candidate generation, not responsibility confirmation, priority judgment, or formal routing.

AI structures information and generates candidates. Humans confirm, route, and own the outcome.

05｜Core Product Responsibility Units

Don’t call everything a requirement. First define what kind of object it is.

In this system, the product design does not label business information as requirements immediately. It first separates information into objects that can move, be confirmed, and be tracked. Signal keeps raw evidence, Candidate Card handles AI candidate generation and human review, and Task enters responsibility routing and status tracking.

Signal is not a requirement. Card is not the final output. Task is what enters organizational action.

Once objects are defined clearly, interface design has a basis: which pages make signals visible, which pages confirm cards, and which pages track tasks.

06｜Key Interfaces / Workflow

Pages are not display layers. They are handoff points in the responsibility flow.

Once the objects are clear, interfaces can have clear responsibility boundaries. The signal pool is not for displaying every piece of feedback; it makes Signals visible and groupable. The card detail page is not for displaying AI results; it lets Candidate Cards be confirmed. The routing and task board is not for showing tasks; it lets Tasks be owned, tracked, and reviewed.

Signal is seen, Card is confirmed, and Task is tracked; only then do pages truly enter the organizational workflow.

07｜Product Architecture Judgment

Don’t rush to connect the model. First turn signals into objects.

Connecting the model directly to CRM, after-sales, meetings, quality, and project systems may expose more information, but the information still lacks unified objects, evidence sources, status, and responsibility boundaries. This project first defines a lightweight business signal layer: different feedback sources become Signals, evidence and context are retained, Candidate Cards are generated, and only human-confirmed objects enter Task.

The signal layer comes first; only then can AI output have object, status, and responsibility.

08｜Product Evolution Path

The loop is not the starting point. The signal flow is the evolution path.

This path is not a feature roadmap. It moves along the places where business signals most often break: first make signals enter a unified entry and retain evidence, then generate candidate cards and confirm them through human review, and finally move into responsibility routing, status tracking, and review feedback.

Product evolution is not about adding features. It is about repairing breakpoints in the signal flow.

09｜Validation / Boundary

Validation is not about proving AI is smart. It is about deciding what cannot be released.

The validation focus is not to prove that AI can generate candidate cards. It is to decide whether each segment can move forward: whether the candidate is wrong, evidence is sufficient, human review is possible, responsibility is clear, and status and review can be retained.

Being able to generate does not mean it can be released. Being released does not mean it can close the loop by itself.

10｜Project Summary

I did not design a requirement generator. I designed a mechanism for signals to enter the responsibility chain.

From business problem, example, readiness judgment, object definition, interface handoff, architecture layer, evolution path, and validation boundary, this project is not about letting AI make judgments for the organization. It is about letting AI, within responsibility boundaries, structure scattered feedback into objects that humans can confirm and move forward.

AI does not make the judgment for the organization. It helps the right judgment appear earlier, be tracked, and be reviewed.
